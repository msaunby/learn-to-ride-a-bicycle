# Lesson 3: Pedalling and Braking

**Learning outcomes.** By the end of this lesson, students can:

- Explain how pedalling helps move a bicycle forward.
- Identify the brake levers and their function on the bicycle used in the lesson, with tutor guidance.
- Describe why slowing early and applying brakes progressively supports a controlled stop.
- Practise a short, comfortable pedal-and-stop sequence, or plan an appropriate alternative.

**Preparation (students):** Bring a bicycle and helmet if available, wear comfortable clothes and closed-toe shoes. Bring your Lesson 2 straight-line note if you have one. Tell the tutor about access needs or concerns before riding.
**Preparation (tutor):** Prepare a flat, wide, traffic-free lane with a generous, unobstructed stopping zone. Identify and demonstrate the brakes on each bicycle; brake layouts can vary. Check equipment and prepare walking, stationary or observation alternatives.

## 1. Review & introduction (5 min)

- **Review:** Recall one thing that helped with a gentle start or straight travel in Lesson 2. If you missed it, name one way you would prepare a clear route.
- **This lesson:** Learn how to pedal smoothly and practise a gentle, progressive stop. The tutor will identify the brakes on the bicycle being used; do not guess which lever operates which brake.

## 2. Mini-lecture (15 min)

### How pedalling moves the bicycle (5 min)

- Pedalling turns the crank and, through the chain, drives the rear wheel on a typical bicycle.
- Use smooth, comfortable strokes rather than sudden bursts.
- Keep looking ahead and hold the grips comfortably. Speed is not the goal.
- A learner can observe or practise stationary pedal movement if riding is not suitable.

### Brake before the stop point (7 min)

- Brake arrangements can vary. The tutor demonstrates the levers on the learner's bicycle and follows the maker's instructions.
- Practise applying the brakes gently and progressively; avoid grabbing a brake suddenly.
- Slow down before the stopping point, then stop in a clear zone and put a foot down.
- Ask the tutor to check the bicycle if a brake feels wrong or its function is unclear. Do not practise on a bicycle with a suspected fault.

### Live demo (3 min)

At low speed in a clear, traffic-free lane, identify each brake lever on the demonstration bicycle. Show smooth pedalling, progressive braking before a marked stop point, and a stable pause after stopping. Ensure learners can see the stopping zone and ask questions before practice.

## 3. Workshop (25 min)

Students work in pairs. The observer stands well outside the lane and offers one practical, supportive note. The tutor supervises every riding activity. Learners can walk, observe or plan the sequence instead.

**Exercise 3.1: Smooth pedalling (8 min).**

In a wide, clear lane, practise a few steady pedal strokes at a comfortable pace, only if ready. A partner watches from a safe place and notes one sign of good control. A learner who is not riding can identify the crank and chain on a stationary bicycle or diagram.

**Exercise 3.2: Brake and stop (10 min).**

With the tutor's guidance, first identify and rehearse the brakes while stationary. If the tutor confirms the bicycle is suitable, practise a gentle stop before a marker at low speed, leaving plenty of stopping room. Save one useful reminder in your portfolio.

**Exercise 3.3: Stop-and-reset (7 min).**

Practise travelling a short distance, stopping in the clear zone, putting a foot down and pausing. Use your Lesson 2 note if available; otherwise make a fresh route check. Record a short stopping plan.

## 4. Self-assessment quiz (7 min)

1. What do the pedals and chain help the bicycle do?
2. Why should you begin braking before the stopping point?
3. True or false: A sudden hard brake is the best way to learn to stop.
4. What should you do once the bicycle has stopped?
5. Name one sign that your practice space is suitable for a stopping exercise.

## 5. Discussion & wrap-up (8 min)

### Discussion

Choose one or two questions. Groups discuss for 3–4 minutes, then take a few answers from the room.

1. What helped you keep a steady rhythm or understand the pedalling action?
2. How did beginning to brake early affect the stop?
3. What would you like to practise again at low speed?

### Key takeaways

- Smooth pedalling at a comfortable pace supports control.
- Identify the brakes on the specific bicycle with the tutor; arrangements can vary.
- Slow early, apply brakes progressively and pause after stopping.

### Next lesson

- **Topic:** Wide turns, noticing hazards and combining the skills.
- **Bring:** Bicycle and helmet if available; bring your braking reminder or stopping plan if you have one.

## Self-study notes

- On a stationary bicycle or diagram, trace how the pedals, crank, chain and rear wheel connect. Do not attempt repairs.
- For Exercise 3.1, describe what a smooth pedal stroke would feel like, or observe a demonstration. For Exercises 3.2 and 3.3, write or draw a plan with a slow-down point, clear stopping zone and pause after stopping. Only practise on a suitable bicycle, in a traffic-free area, with appropriate supervision.
- **Further reading:** Read the bicycle manufacturer's instructions and ask a qualified local cycle instructor or mechanic about brake operation or concerns.
- **Discussion:** write a short paragraph in your portfolio answering one of the discussion questions.

## Tutor notes

### Exercise guidance

- **Exercise 3.1:** The aim is a comfortable, steady rhythm, not distance or speed. The learner can identify the drive parts without riding.
- **Exercise 3.2:** Confirm the brakes and bicycle are suitable before the learner rides. Demonstrate the specific levers and follow manufacturer guidance. A learner who missed Lesson 2 can start with a new clear-lane check; do not rely on the earlier note.
- **Exercise 3.3:** Look for a plan that starts slowing before the marker and finishes in a clear zone. If a learner is not riding, use a sketch or verbal walkthrough.

### Delivery tips

- Brake lever layouts may differ; never assume a standard. If any brake is faulty or unclear, do not ride that bicycle.
- Keep speeds low and stopping distances generous. Do not ask learners to test emergency stops.
- If time is short, prioritise a stationary brake identification and explanation over more riding repetitions.
