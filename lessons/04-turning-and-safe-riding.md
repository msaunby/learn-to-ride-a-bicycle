# Lesson 4: Turning and Safe Riding

**Learning outcomes.** By the end of this lesson, students can:

- Describe how to approach a wide turn at a controlled pace and look through the open route.
- Identify common hazards and ways to leave space for other people.
- Combine a gentle start, straight travel, a wide turn and a controlled stop in a simple traffic-free practice plan.
- Make a personal checklist and choose a suitable next practice goal.

**Preparation (students):** Bring a bicycle and helmet if available, wear comfortable clothes and closed-toe shoes. Bring earlier practice notes if you have them. Tell the tutor about access needs or concerns before riding.
**Preparation (tutor):** Set out a wide, simple circuit with cones, a clear stopping zone and room between learners. Walk and inspect the route for hazards before use. Check equipment and provide a walk-through, walking-only and observation option. Explain that this course is not public-road training.

## 1. Review & introduction (5 min)

- **Review:** Name one useful reminder from the earlier lessons: check the space, look ahead, start gently or slow before stopping. Learners without notes can choose one reminder now.
- **This lesson:** Practise a wide turn only at a pace that feels controlled, notice hazards and put familiar skills together. Riding is optional; walking the course or planning it on paper also counts.

## 2. Mini-lecture (15 min)

### A wide, gentle turn (6 min)

- Walk the route before riding and check that it is flat, wide and clear.
- Slow down before the bend if needed, then look towards the open route through the turn.
- Keep arms relaxed and steer gradually around a broad curve.
- Leave room to stop if the turn does not feel right. Do not practise tight or fast turns.

### Notice hazards and other people (6 min)

Scan for people, uneven ground, loose gravel, puddles and obstacles. Give others space and follow the tutor's directions. If the area is no longer clear, stop and wait for the tutor to reset it.

These lessons use a traffic-free practice area. They do not teach public-road riding, traffic rules or road readiness. Learners need further instruction and local guidance before considering riding in traffic.

### Live demo (3 min)

Walk the course and point out the turning path, any hazards and the stopping zone. Demonstrate a wide curve at a slow, controlled pace, looking through the turn and stopping in the clear zone. Invite learners to identify where they would slow or pause.

## 3. Workshop (25 min)

Students work in pairs. The observer stays outside the route, watches for the planned skill and gives one positive note and one optional practice idea. Do not pressure learners to ride; walking or drawing the route is an alternative.

**Exercise 4.1: Wide turns (8 min).**

Walk the route first. If ready and the tutor confirms it is suitable, practise a gentle turn around widely spaced cones in the traffic-free area. Look through the turn and leave a clear stopping space.

**Exercise 4.2: Skills circuit (10 min).**

Combine a gentle start, straight travel, a wide turn and a controlled stop. A partner observes from outside the route and offers one positive note and one practice idea. Learners can instead walk the route and explain where they would look, slow and stop.

**Exercise 4.3: My safe-riding checklist (7 min).**

Write or draw a personal checklist: bicycle check, helmet, clear space, look ahead, slow before turning and stop safely. Add a confidence note and one small practice goal to your portfolio. Bring forward earlier notes if useful; a fresh checklist is fine.

## 4. Self-assessment quiz (7 min)

1. What should you do before entering a turn?
2. Where should you look while following a curve?
3. True or false: This course's practice circuit prepares you to ride on a public road.
4. Name two things to look for when checking a practice route.
5. Name two items to include in a personal safe-practice checklist.

## 5. Discussion & wrap-up (8 min)

### Discussion

Choose one or two questions. Groups discuss for 3–4 minutes, then take a few answers from the room.

1. What helped you make or plan a smooth, wide curve?
2. What hazard or other person did you notice on the course?
3. What will you practise next, and where can you do it safely?

### Key takeaways

- Walk and check the route; slow before a turn and look through the open path.
- Leave space for people and stop if a hazard or changing condition makes the route unsuitable.
- Build skills gradually in a safe, traffic-free area; seek qualified guidance before riding in traffic.

### Next lesson

- **Topic:** This is the final lesson. Continue with gradual, appropriate practice and seek qualified local instruction for further skills.
- **Bring:** Keep your personal checklist and practice goal. For any future practice, use a suitable bicycle, helmet and traffic-free area, and stop if conditions or confidence change.

## Self-study notes

- Draw a wide curve and mark the point where you would slow, the direction you would look and a clear place to stop. Add hazards to the sketch and show how you would avoid or respond to them by stopping.
- For Exercise 4.1, walk a safe route or trace it on paper. For Exercise 4.2, write or speak through the sequence: prepare, start, look ahead, slow, turn, stop. For Exercise 4.3, make the checklist and goal without needing an earlier portfolio item.
- **Further reading:** Find local cycle training from a qualified provider. Consult local guidance before any public-road riding; this course does not provide road training.
- **Discussion:** write a short paragraph in your portfolio answering one of the discussion questions.

## Tutor notes

### Exercise guidance

- **Exercise 4.1:** A good turn is broad, slow and planned; learners may walk it. Check the cones and surface before each attempt.
- **Exercise 4.2:** The aim is to connect familiar steps, not to assess or certify a learner. Fallback for someone who missed an earlier lesson: walk the circuit and rehearse each step; a fresh route and equipment check replaces missing notes.
- **Exercise 4.3:** The checklist should include preparation, awareness, gradual control and stopping. A confidence note should not be framed as a performance score.

### Delivery tips

- Inspect the course before use and whenever conditions change. Keep waiting learners and observers outside the riding route.
- Avoid any implication that completing a circuit means a learner is ready for public roads. Signpost further instruction and local rules.
- If time is short or the area is busy, replace the circuit with a supervised walk-through and checklist activity.
