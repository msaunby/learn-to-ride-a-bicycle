# Lesson 2: Steering and Starting

**Learning outcomes.** By the end of this lesson, students can:

- Describe a calm sequence for preparing, starting and stopping.
- Explain why looking ahead and making small steering movements can help with straight-line travel.
- Practise a gentle push-off and short glide, or describe an appropriate alternative.
- Identify features of a wide, clear practice lane.

**Preparation (students):** Bring a bicycle and helmet if available, wear comfortable clothes and closed-toe shoes. Bring your Lesson 1 check or confidence note if you have one. Tell the tutor about access needs or concerns before riding.
**Preparation (tutor):** Prepare a flat, wide, traffic-free lane with clear start and stopping zones, marked with cones or chalk. Check equipment and helmet fit. Have a no-riding option ready, such as walking the bicycle or sketching the lane.

## 1. Review & introduction (5 min)

- **Review:** What bicycle or practice-space check from Lesson 1 would you do before starting? Learners without the Lesson 1 note can name one check now.
- **This lesson:** Practise a gentle start, look ahead and use small steering movements. Learners can walk the bicycle or observe instead of riding.

## 2. Mini-lecture (15 min)

### Look where you want to go (6 min)

- Looking towards the open route can help guide where you travel; avoid staring at the front wheel.
- Relax the shoulders and hold the grips without squeezing hard.
- Use small steering adjustments. Sudden movements can make a learner feel less steady.
- Keep the route broad and leave space to slow or stop.

### A simple start sequence (6 min)

Use the learner's bicycle and point out that controls and pedal positions differ. Demonstrate slowly at a pace that feels safe.

1. Choose a clear, flat starting place and check the bicycle, helmet and route.
2. Hold both grips and place one foot on a pedal in a comfortable position.
3. Push off gently with the other foot; bring it to the pedal only when ready.
4. Look ahead and make small steering movements.
5. Slow gently and stop in the clear zone; put a foot down when stopped.

A learner may begin by walking the bicycle or doing a stationary rehearsal. Do not hold or steer a moving bicycle unless the helper is trained and the learner has agreed.

### Live demo (3 min)

In the marked lane, show the clear route and stopping space, then demonstrate a gentle push-off, short straight glide and controlled stop. Repeat slowly and ask learners what they noticed about gaze, shoulders and steering.

## 3. Workshop (25 min)

Students work in pairs. The partner observes from a safe place beside the lane, never in the route, and offers one encouraging observation. Swap roles if both learners want to practise.

**Exercise 2.1: Set-up and push-off (8 min).**

Take turns preparing the bicycle and practising a gentle push-off in a wide lane. A learner may rehearse while stationary or walk the bicycle instead. Record one thing that helped the start feel controlled.

**Exercise 2.2: Follow the line (10 min).**

Walk the route first. If ready, ride or walk the bicycle along a straight lane marked with chalk or cones. Look towards the end and make small corrections. Save a sketch of the lane in your portfolio.

**Exercise 2.3: Start, glide, stop (7 min).**

Repeat the start and glide, then stop in the marked clear zone. Record what helped you feel steady; bring the note to Lesson 3. A learner who is not riding can describe or sketch the sequence.

## 4. Self-assessment quiz (7 min)

1. Where should you look when steering along a straight lane?
2. Name one thing that makes a push-off controlled.
3. True or false: Small steering adjustments are usually easier to control than sudden jerks.
4. What should you do if you feel unsteady?
5. Name two features of a safe starting and stopping space.

## 5. Discussion & wrap-up (8 min)

### Discussion

Choose one or two questions. Groups discuss for 3–4 minutes, then take a few answers from the room.

1. Which part of the start sequence helped most?
2. What happened when you looked towards the end of the lane?
3. What is one small goal for a future practice?

### Key takeaways

- Prepare a wide, clear route and a stopping space before moving.
- Looking ahead can help guide your direction.
- Gentle starts, small steering movements and a calm pace support control.

## Self-study notes

- Use a stationary bicycle or sketch to rehearse the start sequence. If you have appropriate equipment and a safe traffic-free lane, walk the route first and practise only if comfortable.
- For Exercise 2.1, describe or rehearse the steps without moving. For Exercise 2.2, draw a wide lane and mark the start and stopping zones. For Exercise 2.3, write a sequence for starting, looking ahead and stopping.
- **Further reading:** Consult the bicycle and helmet instructions and seek qualified local cycle instruction before attempting a new riding skill.
- **Discussion:** write a short paragraph in your portfolio answering one of the discussion questions.

## Tutor notes

### Exercise guidance

- **Exercise 2.1:** Good practice is calm preparation, a gentle push-off and the option to stop. A learner without a Lesson 1 note should make a fresh bicycle-and-space check; no prior output is required.
- **Exercise 2.2:** The lane must be wide, flat and clear, with the observer outside the route. Fallback for a learner who missed Lesson 1 or has no bicycle: draw a lane and identify where to look and stop.
- **Exercise 2.3:** Look for a clear sequence that includes a controlled stop. The Lesson 2 note is useful in Lesson 3 but not required; learners can start with a fresh check.

### Delivery tips

- Let learners choose between riding, walking, stationary rehearsal and observing. Do not use a narrow course or encourage speed.
- Keep the marked stopping zone clear. Pause if the surface, weather or activity becomes unsuitable.
- If time is short, walk the lane together and practise one gentle start rather than rushing repetitions.
