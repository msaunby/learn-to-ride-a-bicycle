# Lesson 1: Balance and Bike Basics

**Learning outcomes.** By the end of this lesson, students can:

- Name the main parts of a bicycle used in the lesson.
- Describe a simple pre-ride visual check and identify when to ask for help.
- Describe how a correctly fitted helmet and a clear practice area support safer practice.
- Try a walk-and-glide activity, or explain an alternative that feels manageable.

**Preparation (students):** Bring a bicycle and helmet if available, wear comfortable clothes and closed-toe shoes. Tell the tutor about access needs or concerns before riding. It is fine to observe or walk the bicycle instead.
**Preparation (tutor):** Prepare a flat, wide, traffic-free space and a short lane with a clear stopping zone. Check the bicycle and helmet are suitable for the learner; do not ride a bicycle with damage or an unsuitable fit. Have cones, paper and pens ready. Agree on a stop signal and explain that learners can pause or opt out at any time.

## 1. Review & introduction (5 min)

- **Review:** There is no previous lesson. Ask what learners have tried before, if anything, and what would help them feel comfortable. No one needs to disclose experience or ride to take part.
- **This lesson:** Meet the bicycle, identify basic checks and try gentle balance activities in a safe practice space. Set the expectation that stopping or observing is always an acceptable choice.

## 2. Mini-lecture (15 min)

### Meet the bicycle (5 min)

Use a stationary bicycle and invite learners to point out the handlebars, saddle, frame, wheels, pedals, chain and brake levers.

- The handlebars help direct the front wheel; the pedals and chain help drive the bicycle.
- Controls and bicycle designs can differ. The tutor identifies the controls on the bicycle being used.
- Ask for help if a bicycle does not fit, a part seems loose or damaged, or a control is unfamiliar.

### Prepare to practise (7 min)

Demonstrate a simple visual check without suggesting that it replaces maintenance by a qualified bicycle mechanic.

1. Look for obvious tyre damage or a tyre that seems very soft.
2. While stationary, check that the brake levers and brakes appear to work as demonstrated by the tutor.
3. Look for a loose or tangled chain, rubbing wheel or other visible problem.
4. Check that the saddle and handlebars appear secure; ask for help if uncertain.
5. Check helmet fit, footwear and the clear practice area.

- A helmet should sit level and snug, with the straps adjusted according to its instructions. Ask a trusted adult or the tutor to check fit if needed.
- Do not use equipment that appears damaged or unsuitable; ask a qualified person to assess it.
- Choose a flat, wide, traffic-free practice space with room to stop. Never practise towards a road, slope, wall or parked vehicle.

### Live demo (3 min)

With the bicycle stationary, point out its main parts and show the visual check. Walk the practice lane, identify the stopping zone, then demonstrate walking the bicycle and a short gentle glide only if the tutor and venue conditions make it safe. Keep hands away from moving wheels and the chain.

## 3. Workshop (25 min)

Students work in pairs where possible. One person practises while the other observes from a safe place; swap roles. Do not pull, push, hold or steer a moving rider. The tutor supervises and can offer an individual alternative.

**Exercise 1.1: Name and check (8 min).**

Together, point to the bicycle parts and talk through the simple visual check. Draw and label one part or record one check that you will remember. Save it in your portfolio as your bike-and-space check.

**Exercise 1.2: Mount, stand and step (8 min).**

With the bicycle stationary and secured by the learner or tutor, practise standing over it, holding the grips and putting one foot down. Take turns observing. Do not pull or push the learner; the learner decides whether to continue.

**Exercise 1.3: Walk and glide (9 min).**

In the marked lane, first walk the bicycle. If ready, try a short, gentle glide with feet ready to touch down. Stop whenever you want. Record what helped you feel steady, or what alternative activity you chose; bring the note to Lesson 2.

## 4. Self-assessment quiz (7 min)

1. Which part of the bicycle helps you steer?
2. Name two things to look for in a simple pre-ride visual check.
3. True or false: A helmet should sit level on your head.
4. What can you do if you feel wobbly or unsure?
5. What kind of area is suitable for beginner practice?

## 5. Discussion & wrap-up (8 min)

### Discussion

Choose one or two questions. Groups discuss for 3–4 minutes, then take a few answers from the room.

1. Which bicycle part or check was new to you?
2. What helped you feel steady or comfortable during the activity?
3. What would make the next practice feel manageable?

### Key takeaways

- Check the bicycle, helmet and practice area before riding; ask for help if unsure.
- Begin with simple, short activities and choose your own pace.
- Keep your eyes up and your feet ready to touch down during a glide.

### Next lesson

- **Topic:** Gentle starts, looking ahead and steering in a straight line.
- **Bring:** Bicycle and helmet if available, comfortable clothes and closed-toe shoes; bring your bike-and-space check or confidence note if you made one.

## Self-study notes

- Work through the check using a stationary bicycle, or use a labelled bicycle diagram if one is unavailable. Do not attempt mechanical repairs; ask a qualified person about anything that seems wrong.
- For Exercise 1.2, practise standing beside a stationary bicycle or simply describe the steps. For Exercise 1.3, walk the bicycle in a safe, traffic-free place; only glide if you have suitable equipment, space and supervision.
- **Further reading:** Read the bicycle and helmet manufacturers' instructions. Ask a qualified local cycle instructor or bicycle mechanic for advice about fit and condition.
- **Discussion:** write a short paragraph in your portfolio answering one of the discussion questions.

## Tutor notes

### Exercise guidance

- **Exercise 1.1:** Look for accurate naming and a sensible response to uncertainty: stop and ask for help rather than guessing. A learner may draw, write or dictate the portfolio note.
- **Exercise 1.2:** The aim is comfort with the stationary bicycle and controls, not mounting independently. Keep the bicycle stable without restraining the learner.
- **Exercise 1.3:** A short walk is a complete option. Do not pressure anyone to glide. Fallback for learners without a bicycle: label a diagram and sketch a safe practice lane.

### Delivery tips

- Check site permissions, weather, surfaces, equipment fit, supervision and emergency arrangements before the session. Keep the practice lane separate from vehicles and pedestrians.
- Avoid describing a brief visual check as a guarantee that a bicycle is safe or roadworthy. Refer concerns to a qualified mechanic.
- If time is short, prioritise a calm equipment and space check; learners can observe the balance activity.
