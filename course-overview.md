# Learn to Ride a Bicycle: Course Overview

> **Status: DRAFT v0.2.** A practical introduction for beginner riders. Before each delivery, check that the practice area, bicycles, helmets, weather and staffing are suitable. These lessons teach basic control in a traffic-free practice area; they are not public-road training or a substitute for local qualified instruction.

## Aims

By the end of the course, participants will be able to:

1. Identify basic bicycle parts and carry out a simple pre-ride visual check; prepare a suitable helmet and practice space.
2. Practise balance, a gentle start and straight-line steering at a pace that feels comfortable.
3. Pedal steadily and make a controlled stop using the bicycle's brakes as demonstrated by the tutor.
4. Ride a wide, gentle turn, notice hazards and combine the skills in a simple traffic-free practice circuit.

## Audience

This course is for people who are new to riding or are rebuilding basic confidence. No cycling experience is assumed. The course does not assume a particular age or fitness level. Learners can take part at their own pace and may walk the bicycle or observe an activity instead of riding. The tutor should discuss access needs and suitable equipment before practice.

## Format

- **4 lessons × 1 hour**, led by a tutor.
- Students work in pairs or small groups of 2–4 where practical. Activities can also be completed alone or with a tutor.
- Every lesson follows the same structure. The timings are provisional and should be reviewed after a pilot run.

| Segment | Time | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Review & introduction | 5 min | Recall the previous lesson or check in, then introduce the outcomes |
| Mini-lecture | 15 min | Explain core concepts and demonstrate the skill |
| Workshop | 25 min | Practise in a flat, wide, traffic-free area |
| Self-assessment quiz | 7 min | Check understanding, then review answers |
| Discussion & wrap-up | 8 min | Reflect on practice, reinforce takeaways and set a safe next step |

- **Tutor notes:** every lesson includes exercise guidance, delivery tips, a **Your examples** section and a **Pilot findings** section.
- **Self-study route:** each lesson adapts the exercises for one person. Quiz answers are hidden in a collapsible section.
- **Students' guide:** [Ways of working on this course](ways-of-working.md) explains what to bring and how to keep a learning portfolio.
- **Safety boundary:** all riding activities in these plans are for a flat, clear, traffic-free area, under the tutor's supervision. Do not use the lessons to train learners for public-road riding. Follow local guidance, site rules, safeguarding procedures and equipment-manufacturer instructions. Stop an activity if a learner feels unsafe, the conditions change or equipment appears unsuitable.

### How the lessons connect

| Output | Created in | Used again in |
|---|---|---|
| Bike-and-space check and confidence note | Lesson 1, Exercises 1.1 and 1.3 | Lesson 2 review; Lesson 4 safe-riding checklist |
| Straight-lane sketch and steady-start note | Lesson 2, Exercises 2.2 and 2.3 | Lesson 3, Exercise 3.3; Lesson 4 skills circuit |
| Progressive-braking reminder and stopping-zone plan | Lesson 3, Exercises 3.2 and 3.3 | Lesson 4 skills circuit and personal checklist |
| Safe-riding checklist and next practice goal | Lesson 4, Exercise 4.3 | Learner's personal practice plan after the course |

Learners who do not have an earlier portfolio item can make a fresh check or plan at the start of the activity. No exercise depends on a learner having completed a prior ride.

## Assessment

- **Formative:** a five-question self-assessment quiz in each lesson, with answers and short explanations.
- **Practical:** learners make brief written or drawn portfolio notes about checks, practice plans and confidence. They may describe or observe a skill rather than demonstrate it.
- There is no pass/fail test, certificate or assessment of public-road readiness. The tutor should encourage gradual practice and further qualified instruction where needed.

## Materials the tutor needs to prepare

| Lesson | Material |
|---|---|
| All | Flat, wide, traffic-free practice area; suitable bicycles and correctly fitting helmets; closed-toe footwear; water and weather-appropriate clothing; cones or visible markers; first-aid and emergency arrangements appropriate to the venue; paper and pens for portfolio notes; an option to observe or walk the course |
| 1 | Bicycle parts/check demonstration; safe, uncluttered short glide lane |
| 2 | Chalk or cones for a wide, straight lane with a clear stopping zone |
| 3 | A bicycle for the tutor to demonstrate; clearly marked short lane and generous stopping zone |
| 4 | Widely spaced cones for a gentle curve and a simple circuit, with a walk-through route and clear stopping space |

Check fit and equipment before riding. A bicycle with damage, an unsuitable fit or a brake or control that the tutor cannot identify should not be used for riding practice; ask a suitably qualified person to assess it. Do not hold or steer a learner on a moving bicycle unless the helper is trained and the learner has agreed.

## Maintaining the course

- Before every delivery, check local venue rules, weather, the condition and fit of equipment, accessibility arrangements and the appropriate supervision and emergency plan.
- Review safety guidance and any local cycling instruction recommendations at least once a year. Update the course if local guidance changes.
- Keep a changelog at the bottom of this file, and outstanding work in [TODO.md](TODO.md).
- Encourage tutors to replace generic discussion examples with ones that suit the cohort, without changing the safety boundary.
